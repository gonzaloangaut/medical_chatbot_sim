# Common Cold

## Overview and Transmission

The common cold is usually a mild viral infection of the upper respiratory tract, including the nose and throat. Many viruses can cause a cold, with rhinoviruses among the most common. Cold viruses spread through respiratory droplets, close contact, and contact with contaminated surfaces followed by touching the face.

## Symptoms

Typical symptoms include sneezing, a runny or blocked nose, sore throat, cough, and sometimes headache. Symptoms generally begin a few days after infection and may continue for one to two weeks. Similar complaints can also occur with influenza or allergies, so symptoms alone do not always identify the cause.

## Care and Warning Signs

A cold has no specific cure, and antibiotics do not treat cold viruses. Rest, fluids, saline nasal products, and other symptom-relief measures may help. Over-the-counter cold medicines require particular care in children; read labels to avoid taking duplicate ingredients. Medical advice is appropriate for breathing difficulty, dehydration, fever that persists, symptoms that fail to improve, or symptoms that improve and then worsen.

## Prevention

Frequent handwashing, covering coughs and sneezes, cleaning commonly touched surfaces, and limiting close contact when sick reduce opportunities for spread. Avoid touching the eyes, nose, and mouth with unwashed hands. There is no vaccine against the common cold.
