# Fever

## Overview and Body Temperature

A fever means that body temperature is above a person's usual range. A temperature close to 37 °C (98.6 °F) is often described as an average normal temperature, but normal temperature varies among people and throughout the day. A reading of 37 °C by itself does not establish that someone has a fever. Fever is a sign, not a disease; it often accompanies the body's response to an infection.

## Common Causes

Infections caused by viruses or bacteria account for many fevers. Other possible causes include certain medicines, heat-related illness, autoimmune diseases, some cancers, and some childhood vaccines. A temperature reading alone does not identify the cause of a person's symptoms.

## General Care

Care for fever depends on its underlying cause. Drinking enough liquids matters because fever can contribute to fluid loss and dehydration. A health care professional may recommend fever-reducing medicine when appropriate; children with fever should not be given aspirin. A very high fever or symptoms that are concerning warrant medical advice.
