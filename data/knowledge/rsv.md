# Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV) Infections

## Overview and Transmission

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a common virus that infects the respiratory tract. Infection often resembles a cold, but RSV can also cause serious lower respiratory illness. It spreads through coughs or sneezes, close contact, and contact with contaminated objects followed by touching the eyes, nose, or mouth.

## Symptoms and Higher Risk

RSV symptoms may include a runny nose, cough, sneezing, reduced appetite, fever, and wheezing. Symptoms do not always appear at the same time. Very young infants may instead show irritability, decreased activity, or breathing difficulties. Infants, older adults, people with certain heart or lung conditions, and people with weakened immune systems are more likely to develop severe infection, including bronchiolitis or pneumonia.

## Diagnosis

A health care professional may evaluate RSV through symptoms, medical history, and physical examination. Testing a sample from the nose or another respiratory site can help identify RSV, especially in severe cases. Additional tests may be used to assess complications. Similar symptoms can be caused by other respiratory infections.

## Care and Prevention

Many RSV infections resolve without specific antiviral treatment; supportive care and adequate fluids are important. Severe cases may require hospital care and breathing support. Handwashing, covering coughs, reducing close contact when ill, and cleaning surfaces help limit spread. Vaccines and preventive antibody medicines are available for certain groups; eligibility should be discussed with a health care professional because recommendations depend on age, pregnancy, and other factors.
