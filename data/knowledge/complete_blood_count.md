# Complete Blood Count (CBC)

## Overview and Measurements

A complete blood count (CBC), also called a full blood count or hemogram, is a set of blood measurements. It includes red blood cells, which carry oxygen; white blood cells, which help defend against infections; and platelets, which help blood clot. A CBC can also report hemoglobin, hematocrit, and average red blood cell size (MCV). A CBC with differential provides additional detail about types of white blood cells.

## Purpose

A CBC may be ordered during a routine checkup or to investigate symptoms. It can help clinicians evaluate conditions such as anemia, infection, immune system disorders, and blood diseases. It can also be used to monitor certain illnesses or treatments. The reason for ordering a CBC depends on the clinical situation.

## Procedure and Preparation

A clinician or laboratory professional usually collects a small blood sample from a vein in the arm. The collection typically takes only a few minutes and can cause a brief sting or minor bruising. A CBC by itself generally needs no special preparation. However, fasting may be required when other blood tests are ordered at the same time; patients should follow their provider's instructions.

## Interpreting Results

A CBC can show blood cell counts or related measurements outside a laboratory's reference range. Abnormal values may have many explanations, including health conditions, medicines, and changes in hydration. A CBC alone does not establish a diagnosis. A health care professional interprets the numbers together with symptoms, history, and possibly other tests.
