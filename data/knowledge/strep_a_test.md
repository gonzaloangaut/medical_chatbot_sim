# Strep A Test

## Overview and Test Types

A strep A test looks for group A Streptococcus bacteria, which can cause strep throat. Strep throat is a bacterial infection of the throat and tonsils. Rapid antigen tests provide results quickly, while throat cultures take longer and can detect some infections that a rapid test misses. These tests are different from blood tests such as a complete blood count.

## When Testing May Be Needed

Clinicians may consider strep testing for a sudden severe sore throat, pain with swallowing, fever, swollen neck lymph nodes, or inflamed tonsils. Cough and runny nose are more suggestive of a viral infection than strep throat. Symptoms alone do not reliably distinguish every viral sore throat from a strep infection.

## Sample Collection and Preparation

A health care professional collects a sample by swabbing the back of the throat and tonsils. This can cause brief discomfort or gagging. No special preparation is usually needed for a rapid strep test or throat culture. Rapid testing generally returns results in minutes, while cultures typically take one to two days.

## Results and Follow-up

A positive strep A test indicates that group A streptococcal bacteria were detected. When a rapid test is negative but clinical suspicion remains, the clinician may order a throat culture; follow-up testing is particularly important in children and teenagers. A confirmed symptomatic strep throat infection is generally treated with prescribed antibiotics. Patients should discuss their test results and treatment instructions with their clinician.
