# Heat Illness

## Overview and Risk Factors

Heat illness can develop when the body cannot release heat effectively, particularly during hot or humid weather or strenuous work and exercise. Young children, older adults, and people with certain health conditions are more vulnerable. Some medicines and alcohol use can also increase risk.

## Heat Cramps and Heat Exhaustion

Heat cramps are painful muscle spasms that may occur during heavy activity in hot conditions. Heat exhaustion can follow prolonged heat exposure and inadequate fluid intake. Possible features include heavy sweating, rapid breathing, and a rapid but weak pulse. Without appropriate attention, heat exhaustion may progress to heat stroke.

## Heat Stroke

Heat stroke is a life-threatening heat-related emergency in which body temperature can rise dangerously. Warning signs may include confusion, dizziness, nausea, abnormal skin findings, and a rapid pulse. Suspected heat stroke requires immediate emergency medical help; it is not equivalent to an ordinary infectious fever.

## Prevention

Reduce time spent in extreme heat, take breaks when exercising or working outdoors, and drink enough fluids to avoid dehydration. Replacing fluids and minerals lost through heavy sweating can be important. Risk is greater when heat and humidity interfere with normal cooling.
