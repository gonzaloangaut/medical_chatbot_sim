# Influenza (Flu)

## Overview and Spread

Influenza, often called the flu, is a respiratory infection caused by influenza viruses. People can spread these viruses when coughing, sneezing, or talking. The illness ranges from mild to serious; influenza should not be confused with the digestive illness sometimes called "stomach flu."

## Symptoms and Differences from a Cold

Flu symptoms often start abruptly and may include fever or chills, cough, sore throat, headache, body aches, and marked tiredness. A runny or stuffy nose can also occur. Children sometimes experience vomiting or diarrhea. Compared with a common cold, influenza more often begins suddenly and causes substantial fever, aches, and fatigue; these patterns do not establish a diagnosis on their own.

## Complications and Higher Risk

Influenza can cause pneumonia and other complications or worsen existing medical conditions. Older adults, young children, pregnant people, and people with certain chronic diseases are at higher risk of serious complications. A person who is very ill, has significant concerns, or belongs to a high-risk group should contact a health care professional.

## Treatment and Prevention

Many people recover with rest and supportive care. A clinician may prescribe antiviral medicines, particularly for higher-risk patients; these medicines work best when started early in the illness. Annual influenza vaccination and habits such as handwashing and covering coughs help reduce infection and spread.
