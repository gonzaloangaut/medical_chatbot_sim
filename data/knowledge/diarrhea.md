# Diarrhea

## Overview and Causes

Diarrhea involves loose, watery bowel movements, often defined as three or more in one day. Acute diarrhea is usually short-lived, while diarrhea lasting at least four weeks is considered chronic. Possible causes include infections, contaminated food or water, medicines, food intolerances, and digestive diseases.

## Associated Symptoms and Dehydration

Diarrhea may occur with abdominal cramps, urgent bowel movements, fever, or chills. Fluid loss through diarrhea can lead to dehydration. This is particularly concerning for infants, older adults, and people with weakened immune systems. Symptoms shared with other illnesses do not by themselves establish the cause of diarrhea.

## When to Seek Medical Care

Medical assessment is appropriate for signs of dehydration, blood or pus in stools, black stools, severe abdominal or rectal pain, high fever, or diarrhea that continues longer than expected. Newborns and infants may become dehydrated quickly, so caregivers should contact a health care professional promptly when concerned. Prolonged diarrhea may require investigation of an underlying condition.

## Treatment and Prevention

Treatment focuses on replacing lost fluids and electrolytes and addressing the underlying cause when necessary. Oral rehydration solutions are especially relevant for children. A clinician may request stool or blood tests when symptoms suggest a particular infection or an ongoing disorder. Safe food and drinking water practices can reduce some causes of infectious diarrhea.
